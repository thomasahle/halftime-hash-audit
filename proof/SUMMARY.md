All four 64-bit HalftimeHash wrapper algorithms have a rigorous 63-bit certificate in the requested eight-byte-word score, using their actual overlapping keys, for lengths below 2,761,050,384 bytes times 1, 2, 4, or 8 for Style64, Style128, Style256, or Style512 respectively: the overlap reaches only the length tables, and the output tables remain independent. Encode2 has distance exactly 2, and exact minor certificates give the corrected equal-length core bound `2^-64` below a leaf and `(h+2)(h+5)2^-64` at or above it, where `h` is the base-eight logarithm of the complete leaf count rounded down. The reachable 24-byte `advanced::Vj<3>` APIs are different: for block width `b` in 64-bit words, two `168b`-byte messages, all zero except that one has word `6b` equal to 1, collide with probability at least `2^-32` by exact key-fibre counting, refuting both the valuation-corrected `65*2^-96` formula and the paper's >83-bit headline. These are results for the shipped flat-address, modular-arithmetic function with a fully uniform key array; the literal header is not portable C++ because of its table indexing, and its unmodified NEON dispatch fails to compile, so native NEON verification required disclosed dispatch aliases and modular-overflow semantics.
