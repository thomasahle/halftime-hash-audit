# Proof audit of the “proven” hashes

Audited 2026-09-17/18. **Several advertised claims cannot be certified from the supplied proofs.** In particular, Polymur's numerical headline and almost-pairwise-independence proof, UMASH's reduction-to-prime-field step, and HalftimeHash's multiplication of tree probabilities require corrections or new arguments. The three-key recurrence and ChainHash collision theorem survive the algebraic audit. This does **not** certify their seeded implementations.

The table concerns equality of the **entire specified output**, for every fixed distinct message pair, with the key sampled independently of that pair. `q = 2^64`; `L` is a positive integer number of 8-byte words. For a byte-string family, the length cap is `8L` bytes. Fixed-length families are compared separately at each length. `clip(e) = min(1,e)` is implicit. Numerical scores marked **conditional** are arithmetic on an unverified claim, not certified security levels. Every section below gives the precise domain and minimizing length.

## Audit conventions, sources, and reproducibility

The paper's actual score definition in [paper/injective.tex](paper/injective.tex), immediately before `tab:injective:adversarial`, floors ε at `2^-r` for an `r`-bit output. Accordingly this audit scores

`e(L) = max(2^-r, min(1, certified_bound(L)))`,

then minimizes `log2(L/e(L))` over the **specified domain**, not just benchmark sizes. This floor is a scoring convention, not a mathematical lower bound on every pair's collision probability. If the user-intended score omits the floor, the distinctions are given below, notably for one-word Horner. The empty string can be in a message domain but `L=0` cannot be a score argument. “At most” and “exactly” the length are not interchangeable. For intrinsically paired hashes, admitting `L=1` requires an explicit injective fixed-length padding convention. Scores over arbitrary fractional byte lengths would differ from the integer-word scores in this report.

AU means `Pr[H(m)=H(m')]≤ε`. AXU means `Pr[H(m) xor H(m')=δ]≤ε` for **every fixed** bit string δ. ADU means `Pr[H(m)−H(m')=δ]≤ε` in a specified additive group. In a binary field, field-additive ADU and AXU coincide. In an integer ring they do not. Exact pairwise independence means every output pair has probability `|R|^-2`; an AU statement alone does not imply this.

Three repeatedly used proof facts:

1. A nonzero univariate polynomial of degree d over a field has at most d roots: divide by `(X−a)` at a root and induct on d. A uniform key in a subset K therefore hits its roots with probability at most `d/|K|`; under an arbitrary distribution the valid replacement is `d·max_k Pr[K=k]`. Polynomial **injectivity over integers** is insufficient; it must survive coefficient reduction into the field.
2. For a nonzero multivariate polynomial of total degree d, write it as a polynomial in one variable with leading coefficient of degree at most `d−j`. That coefficient vanishes with probability at most `(d−j)/q`; otherwise the remaining variable has at most j roots. Independence gives `Pr[zero]≤(d−j)/q+j/q`. Unequal key-set sizes require their actual denominators (a safe bound is `d/min |K_i|`). A nonzero formal polynomial can still be the zero function when d is large; capping the probability at 1 is essential.
3. Independent composition gives `e_F+(1−e_F)e_G≤e_F+e_G`: condition on the first-stage outputs. Reusing a key across **positions in one vector output** needs no union factor: equality of the whole vector implies equality at any one fixed differing position. Multiplying probabilities, however, requires independence or an explicit conditional-probability proof.

## 1. CLHASH and CLNH

**Primary source:** Lemire–Kaser, [arXiv:1503.03465](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03465), §§4–5, Lemmas 4–5 and 9, Algorithm 4; [saved full text](gathered/clhash/clhash_arxiv_reading.txt). Implementation distinctions use [upstream `clhash.c`](https://github.com/lemire/clhash/blob/master/src/clhash.c) and the [saved excerpt](gathered/check-clhash/upstream_clhash_c_excerpt.txt).

**1. Precise statement.** CLNH on an even fixed number of 64-bit words is the XOR sum of unreduced carryless products `(m_2i xor k_2i)(m_(2i+1) xor k_(2i+1))`. Every key word is independent uniform. Its unreduced 128-bit output is `2^-64`-AXU; its reduction modulo `X^64+X^4+X^3+X+1` is exactly XOR-universal on 64 bits. An odd fixed word count can be zero padded to an even count. This does not by itself encode variable length.

CLHASH Algorithm 4 adds independently keyed byte length, uses 128-word blocks, and for more than one block applies a polynomial over `GF(2^127)` followed by an independently keyed two-word CLNH reduction. Its ideal key is 128 uniform 64-bit words, a uniform 126-bit polynomial key, a uniform 128-bit final-compressor key, and a uniform 64-bit length key. For unequal byte lengths the unmodified algorithm is `1/q`-AXU. For equal lengths ≤1024 bytes it is `1/q`-AXU. For equal longer lengths, with `n=ceil(bytes/1024)`, the bound is `2/q+(n−1)/2^126`. Over `<2^64` bytes this is less than `2.004/q`.

**2. Re-derivation and qualifications.**

1. Choose a differing word in a CLNH pair. Expand the difference of the two hashes. All key-key terms cancel. Condition on every key except the key paired with the differing word. The remaining equation is `δk=C` with `δ≠0`. In `F_2[X]` multiplication by δ is injective, so at most one of q choices works. After irreducible field reduction it is a bijection, so exactly one works for every C. This proves the full-width claim as well as the reduced claim; the output being 128 bits does **not** give a `2^-128` bound.
2. Each carryless 64×64 product, and their XOR, has degree at most 126. Thus a block result embeds injectively into `GF(2^127)` modulo `X^127+X+1`. If the messages differ in a block, equality of all block results is contained in that block's collision event, at most `1/q`. Block-key reuse is harmless for this containment argument.
3. Conditional on distinct block-result sequences, the polynomial difference is nonzero of degree ≤`n−1`. The 126-bit evaluation set has **exactly `2^126` points**, so its root bound is `(n−1)/2^126`; uniformity over the entire 127-bit field is unnecessary.
4. Lazy reduction modulo `X(X^127+X+1)` preserves congruence modulo the field polynomial. Equality of the lazy representatives therefore implies equality in the field. This is the direction required by the bound. Algorithm 4's 126-bit key has degree at most 125, sufficient for the stated fast fold; surrounding prose about degree at most 126 is an off-by-one description of the selected key space.
5. Conditional on unequal 128-bit intermediate representatives, the independently keyed final CLNH is AXU with probability `1/q`. The independent composition sum is `1/q+(n−1)/2^126+1/q`.
6. For different lengths, condition on all keys except the length key. Multiplication of that key by the nonzero field element encoding the XOR of lengths is a permutation. Thus every output XOR difference occurs with probability `1/q`, even across the short/long branches. This requires distinct 64-bit length encodings. The paper's inclusive `2^64` endpoint does not have such an encoding; use `<2^64` bytes or prove that endpoint separately. A sentence near Lemma 9 claiming `1/q` irrespective of length is too broad for equal long messages; the lemma's actual long-message bound is the one above.
7. A common final bijection preserves **equality** but generally not AXU. The reference `CLHASH_BITMIX` code applies `fmix64` only in the short branch. It preserves same-branch collision bounds, but the paper's simple bijection explanation does not prove the `1/q` cross-branch statement. There is a repair for AU: conditional on all but the independent long-branch final-compressor keys, any specified field product value has probability at most `2/q−1/q²`. Thus the global `2.004/q` AU envelope survives for ideal keys, while the precise cross-length AXU claim is not inherited by this modified implementation.
8. The reference initializer expands a small seed with xorshift128plus and rejects a special polynomial-key pattern. That is not Algorithm 4's product distribution. Neither a bijective output mixer nor SMHasher repairs this missing key-distribution theorem.

**3. Enumeration.** For 2-bit operands, all 16 messages and all 16 keys give maximum AU and AXU probability **1/4**, both before reduction and in `GF(4)`. Both production irreducible polynomials pass independent Rabin tests: `X^(2^64)=X` and `gcd(X^(2^32)−X,Π64)=1`; for degree 127, `X^(2^127)=X` and `gcd(X²−X,Π127)=1`. Horner and composition components are checked separately; no production-size CLHASH enumeration is claimed.

**4. Score and verdict.** CLHASH scores **64 at L=1**. For `L>128`, the denominator grows so slowly that `L/ε(L)>q`; each block-boundary minimum is also above q. CLNH has the same score if the length-one padded family is included; its literal even-word domain starts at L=2 and scores **65**. **The ideal bounds hold.** Publish the domain and distinguish raw Algorithm 4, BITMIX, and the seed generator.

## 2. PolymurHash 2.0

**Primary sources:** [README](https://github.com/orlp/polymur-hash), [universality proof](https://github.com/orlp/polymur-hash/blob/master/extras/universality-proof.md), [implementation](https://github.com/orlp/polymur-hash/blob/master/polymur-hash.h); [refetched proof](audit-support/polymur-proof-current.md).

**1. Precise corrected statement.** Let `p=2^61−1` and

`K = { k∈F_p*: ord(k)=p−1 and canonical(k^7 mod p)<2^60−2^56 }`.

For **uniform k on this exact set**, fixed tweak, and distinct byte strings of at most n bytes, the supplied polynomial construction has collision probability at most `(14+n/7)/|K|`. The shipped full output is 64 bits, not a 61-bit truncated tag. A sharper elementary degree bound valid for this at-most-length domain is

- `D(n)=2` for `1≤n≤7`;
- `D(n)=9` for `8≤n≤21`;
- `D(n)=13` for `22≤n≤49`;
- `D(n)=7 floor((n−1)/49)+14` for `n≥50`.

Thus `clip(D(n)/|K|)` is a corrected ideal AU bound. The public numerical estimate `n·2^-60.2` is **not proved** by discarding 14. The stated joint-output bound `n·2^-124.2` is also **not proved**.

**2. Re-derivation and errors.**

1. In each nonfinal 49-byte block the seven decoded 56-bit values occupy distinct nonconstant coefficients. Expanding the four products gives coefficients, from degrees 7 down to 1, `m6+3, m0, m2, m4, m5, m3, m1`; the constant is `m0m1+m2m3+m4m5`. Block Horner multiplication shifts older terms by 7. Decode the oldest block from its seven highest coefficients, subtract its **whole** contribution, and continue. The highest coefficient cannot vanish modulo p because `3≤m6+3<2^56+3<p`.
2. Multiplication by `k^14` separates this prefix from the tail. The three tail forms have degrees 3, 9 and 13. The short form exposes `m0` at degree 2 and tail length at degree 1. The middle form exposes `m0,m2,m1,length` at degrees 7,3,2,1. In the large tail, decode `m0,m6,m1,m2` at degrees 11,9,6,3, then subtract their known products to decode `m3,m5,m4` at degrees 7,4,2. The length is again at degree 1. All directly decoded data values are below p, so this is injectivity **over F_p**, including across lengths. Overlapping byte reads cause no problem only because their coverage permits recovery of every byte once the tail length is known.
3. Distinct messages consequently give a nonzero difference polynomial. Root counting gives the displayed corrected bounds. For tails ≤7 the common cubic term cancels, giving degree 2. At n=8, comparing different tail branches can leave a degree-9 difference. For full blocks the degree is `7 floor((n−1)/49)+14`; this explains the sharper piecewise D. Removing a positive term from an upper bound is invalid, irrespective of whether it is small asymptotically.
4. The README's `φ(p−1)(2^60−2^56)/p ≈ 2^57.4028` is a **density estimate**, not an exact cardinality or established lower bound. It presumes an unproved distribution of seventh powers of primitive roots in an interval. In particular, 7 divides p−1; this is not a permutation of all nonzero field elements. This audit does not promote the estimate to a certified denominator.
5. The initializer walks deterministically through a 64-bit seed sequence until an acceptable exponent and seventh power are found. Uniform initial seeds do not make successive rejection trials independent, or accepted keys uniform. Accepted states collect all predecessor seeds in their rejection intervals. Even the exponent mapping has endpoint/multiplicity issues. For the shipped distribution the root argument yields only `D(n)·max_k Pr[k]`; no bound for that maximum is supplied. A one-seed convenience initializer can preserve the same marginal k distribution because its seed mixer is a permutation, but cannot turn that distribution into uniform K.
6. The ASU argument fixes s to count roots of a polynomial containing `imix(x−s)` and `imix(y−s)`, then chooses s as a function of k and incorrectly retains the original root count. The polynomial changes with s. Moreover, the displayed rearrangement for s has its sign reversed: it should be `s=y−mix(PH(m')) mod 2^64`. Correct counting is
   `Pr[H(m)=x,H(m')=y] = 2^-64 Pr_k[mix(PH(m))−mix(PH(m'))=x−y]`.
   AU establishes this only for `x=y`. A non-linear permutation need not preserve additive differential bounds. Independent uniform s makes each marginal uniform; it does **not** by itself prove almost pairwise independence.
7. The code does lazy reductions and feeds a noncanonical integer representative to its bijective mixer. Equality of valid bounded representatives implies congruence modulo p, which is enough for the upper bound; it is not necessarily equivalent to equality of canonical residues. The proof's overflow checker is evidence about that implementation, not a formal equivalence proof. The same tweak must be used in both evaluations.

**3. Enumeration.** A short-tail analogue with p=127, 2-bit characters and lengths 0–3 has **18** admissible keys, versus density estimate **17.007874**. Across all 85 messages its exact maximum is **2/18=1/9**, meeting the degree-2 bound. A deterministic rejection-walk analogue maps 1024 seeds to these 18 keys with preimage counts ranging from **16 to 135**, not the uniform 56.8889.

The invalid ASU root-count implication has a direct finite-field counterexample: over F5 take `f(k)=k`, `g(k)=2k`, and permutation `[4,0,1,3,2]`. With independent uniform additive s modulo 5, output pair `(0,1)` occurs for **3/25** keys `(k,s)`, although every fixed-s difference polynomial is nonzero of degree 1. The invalid argument would give 1/25. This is a counterexample to the proof step, not an asserted collision attack on 64-bit Polymur.

**4. Score and verdict.** The retained source bound gives **`log2|K|−log2(106/7)` at L=1**, approximately **53.4822** using the unproved key-density estimate. The sharper D bound gives **`log2|K|−log2 9` at L=1**, approximately **54.2329**. For `L≥2`, `D(8L)≤14+8L/7≤9L`, proving that minimum; probability clipping cannot lower it. The reported 57-bit headline is not justified by this proof. **Ideal AU holds with correction; a rigorous numerical |K| bound, shipped-key distribution bound, and ASU proof are missing.**

## 3. UMASH-64

**Primary source:** Paul Khuong, [UMASH reference and whitepaper](https://github.com/backtrace-labs/umash), 2022-02-23, especially PDF pp.3–6 and key generation on pp.13–15; [saved PDF](gathered/umash/umash.pdf), [refetched literate source](audit-support/umash-current.py). The older 2020 blog's constants are superseded.

**1. Proposed statement.** Full 64-bit equality for arbitrary distinct strings of at most s bytes is claimed to have probability `<ceil(s/4096)2^-55`. The formal model needs independent 64-bit OH key words and an independent polynomial multiplier in `F_(2^61−1)`. The reference key generator actually excludes multipliers 0 **and 1** (`1<m<p`) and samples 34 mutually distinct OH words. Thus the reference distribution is not literally the independent product distribution used in the sketch. A fixed optional 64-bit “seed” parameter is not the theorem's random key.

**2. Re-derivation: what works and where it stops.**

1. PH is unreduced CLNH on one pair, hence `q^-1`-AXU by §1. NH on one pair is `q^-1`-ADU for addition modulo `q²`, by §5 below. Adding a length tag inside NH gives ENH: if data differ, solve the NH difference equation; if data agree and tags differ, equality is impossible. This establishes ENH's AU bound on `(data,tag)`.
2. OH XORs PH values for all but the last 16-byte chunk, with the last chunk processed by ENH. At equal chunk count, a differing PH chunk supplies an independent AXU difference and absorbs the rest into a fixed target. If only the last chunk/tag differs, all PH terms cancel and ENH supplies AU. Either case is at most `1/q`.
3. At unequal chunk counts, condition on all keys except the longer block's final pair. Its integer product must equal one specified 128-bit value, after removing its fixed tag and XOR offset. A nonzero factor gives at most one other factor; a zero factor contributes the exceptional zero-product case. The largest point mass is at most `(2q−1)/q²<2/q`, attained at zero. Thus **raw 128-bit OH is `<2/q`-AU**, with `1/q` for equal chunk counts. This argument is valid and needs no independent key per message block.
4. The sketch then reduces each 64-bit half of OH modulo `p=2^61−1`, describing the result as losing approximately six bits and asserting an AU bound around `2^-57`. **This implication is not justified.** A many-to-one map does not multiply an AU collision bound by its maximum fibre size. Even AXU does not justify arbitrary modular projection, since XOR differences and integer congruence have different algebra. Neither the needed conditional output distribution nor an appropriate differential bound after the XOR/NH mixture is proved. Each half has up to **9**, not exactly 8, representatives of a residue; joint probabilities cannot be certified by subtracting six “entropy bits.”
5. If the two resulting coefficient sequences are different, the positive-power Horner difference has degree at most `2 ceil(s/256)` and the root bound is that degree divided by the actual multiplier-set size. But the unproved probability that the coefficient sequences coincide is exactly what must be added. Different numbers of blocks can also yield the same formal polynomial if leading coefficients vanish; a bound on this event is needed. Cross-short/long cases require the same care if a long polynomial is identically the relevant constant.
6. With a **proved** coefficient-collision bound sufficiently below `2^-55`, the rounding at 16 blocks could indeed yield the headline. The sketch's rounding alone does not prove the missing coefficient bound. Code arithmetic modulo `2^64−8=8p` is not itself a problem if equality implies congruence modulo p, as claimed; that implication does not repair step 4.
7. Rejection of repeated OH words can be accounted for by conditioning an IID model: divide a valid event bound by `Pr[all 34 words distinct]≥1−561/q`. Restricting the polynomial key replaces p by p−2. These changes must appear in exact denominators and probabilities; short-string probabilities no longer equal the IID formulas. A Salsa20 expansion is a computational model, not full information-theoretic independence.
8. The final map `v xor rotl(v,8) xor rotl(v,33)` really is a permutation. Represent rotation by X in `F_2[X]/(X^64−1)`. Its multiplier is `1+X^8+X^33`; at X=1 it is 1, so it is coprime to `X^64−1=(X+1)^64` and has a multiplicative inverse. Thus this last step preserves equality. For ≤8-byte strings, the decoder is injective at a fixed length, right-xor shifts are triangular invertible maps, and each odd integer multiplier is invertible modulo `2^64`. A uniform length-indexed noise word makes the short output uniform. Independent noise words give collision `1/q` across distinct short lengths in the IID model. This verifies the local short branch and finalizer; it does not resolve the cross-branch or projection gaps.

**3. Enumeration and gap witness.** NH and PH tiny checks give their exact `1/4` bounds. The prime-projection inference is false even for AXU: for five equiprobable keys let `u∈{1,2,4,8,16}`, `H(x)=u`, `H(y)=u+31`, with 6-bit outputs. The five XOR differences are distinct, so this two-message family is `1/5`-AXU. Reducing modulo 31 makes the messages collide with probability **1**, although the projection's largest fibre has size 3. The supposed fibre-scaled bound would be 3/5. This does not establish a violation by UMASH; it shows why a separate proof is necessary. No feasible scaled instance can validate the omitted production argument just by passing.

**4. Score and verdict.** Arithmetic on the **claimed** formula gives **55 at L=1**: `ceil(L/512)≤L`, so `L/ceil(L/512)≥1`. The complete end-to-end bound **could not be verified**. Verified raw OH and the root-counting step must not be presented as a verified composition through prime projection.

## 4. UMASH-128 fingerprint

**Primary source:** the same [whitepaper](gathered/umash/umash.pdf), pp.7–12, and [reference source](https://github.com/backtrace-labs/umash/blob/master/umash_reference.py).

**1. Proposed statement.** The output is the ordered pair of two 64-bit UMASH values, with independent polynomial multipliers but shared PH/NH work. The claim is `<ceil(s/2^26)^2·2^-83` for strings of at most s bytes. This is not the square of UMASH-64's bound. The second compressor uses lane-wise linear shufflers and a separately keyed PH of a checksum.

**2. Re-derivation and gaps.**

1. The checksum is the XOR of input chunks and their key chunks. With equal chunk count, equal checksums force at least two differing original chunks. With unequal count, checksum equality fixes one previously unused random 128-bit chunk and has probability `2^-128` in the IID model.
2. If checksums differ, condition on the original OH keys. The fresh checksum-PH keys make a secondary collision at most `2^-64`, even if the primary collides. For unequal chunk counts, split on checksum equality: its probability contributes at most `2^-128`; otherwise multiply the primary bound by `2^-64`. This justifies the sketch's loose raw-compressor easy-case bound below `2^-126`; independence of the two finished hashes is not being assumed.
3. If exactly two original chunks differ, one PH and the final ENH, simultaneous **raw equality** forces `(I+s_j)Δ_PH=0`. The xor-shift map `I+s_j` is invertible because it is identity plus strictly left-shifting terms (a finite geometric-series inverse exists). Hence both raw differences are zero. Their disjoint key pairs give probability at most `2^-128`.
4. With two differing PH chunks, write the two collision equations as `Δ_i xor Δ_j=y` and `s_i(Δ_i) xor s_j(Δ_j)=z`, after conditioning on all other original chunks. Equal checksum contributions cancel identically. Eliminate Δ_i. The kernel of `s_i+s_j` on two 64-bit lanes has size at most `2^30`, since at most 15 high bits per lane can disappear. PH's top bit is always zero, reducing the possible kernel intersection to at most `2^29`. Each specified Δ_j has probability ≤`2^-64`; union over those candidates gives `2^-35`. Conditional on it, the independent Δ_i must hit one value, adding `2^-64`. Thus the raw joint equality bound **`2^-99` is justified**. The text's reference to “2^29 bits” should be “2^29 possible values.”
5. The next step turns `2^-99` into approximately `2^-87` by projecting four 64-bit halves into the 61-bit prime field. **The same missing projection argument as §3 remains.** A bound for raw joint equality is insufficient to bound simultaneous equalities after four non-injective, non-XOR-compatible reductions. In subcase 3 above the ENH argument only handles the zero difference forced there; it does not prove the full family of differential equations required by a lifting argument.
6. If coefficient-level single bounds B1,B2 and joint bound B12 were available, the valid conditional composition would be
   `Pr[collision]≤B12+(B1+B2)d/(p−2)+(d/(p−2))²`,
   with `d≤2 ceil(s/256)` and separate treatment of any unequal-degree/short cases. This follows by partitioning into both coefficient streams equal, exactly one equal, and neither equal, then conditioning on all compressor keys. Two independent polynomial keys justify the final square. The sketch's middle term also uses a single-compressor constant inconsistent with its earlier reduced bound; it must be corrected and the final rounding recalculated after B1,B2,B12 are proved.
7. For IID short-string keys, equal-length distinct messages never collide; different lengths give `2^-128` through two independent constraints, even when a key word is shared between constraints. Distinct-word rejection changes exact counts. The full seeded 38-word parameter state is again not independent random material.

**3. Enumeration.** The PH/NH components and the projection counterexample are in the executable suite. The joint shuffler kernel argument above is a linear-algebra count, not a claim that a production fingerprint was exhaustively tested. No end-to-end numerical check supplied here closes the missing projection lemma.

**4. Score and verdict.** For the claimed formula, put `A=2^23` words and `j=ceil(L/A)`. Within each plateau ε is constant, so only `L=1` and `L=(j−1)A+1` need consideration. At successive starts with `j≥2`, the ratio is proportional to `(A(j−1)+1)/j²`, and decreases (strictly after the initial boundary). Therefore a finite domain's minimum is the smaller of its first and last relevant plateau-start scores.

- Up to **64 MiB**, the conditional score is **83 at L=1**.
- With byte lengths `<2^64`, take full-word caps through `2^61−1`. The last plateau has `j=2^38` and starts at **`L*=2^61−2^23+1 = 2305843009205305345`**. The score is exactly **`7+log2(L*)`**, slightly below **68**, not 83.
- Without a length cap, but capping probabilities at 1, the minimum is near ε's saturation at `L≈2^64.5`, about **64.5 bits**. Leaving a quadratic “probability” above 1 and taking an unbounded minimum is invalid.

**Could not verify the claimed fingerprint theorem; the global 83-bit score additionally fails even as arithmetic on that claim unless a length domain is specified.**

## 5. NH, Toeplitz NH, and UMAC/UHASH

**Primary sources:** Black–Halevi–Krawczyk–Krovetz–Rogaway, [UMAC full paper](https://www.fastcrypto.org/umac/umac-full.pdf), 1999, Theorems 4.2, 4.5, 5.1 and Proposition 6.1; [saved full text](gathered/vmac-nh-umac/umac-full.txt). For the later construction, [RFC 4418](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4418), §§5–6, and Krovetz's [2000 dissertation](https://web.cs.ucdavis.edu/~rogaway/umac/umac_thesis.pdf), Chapter 6, [downloaded copy](audit-support/umac-thesis.pdf). These are **different versions** of UMAC.

**1. Precise statements.** For `Q=2^w`, NH sums `((m_2i+k_2i) mod Q)((m_(2i+1)+k_(2i+1)) mod Q)` modulo Q². The key consists of independent uniform w-bit words, one for each allowed message position. On equal, positive, even word counts up to the key capacity, NH is `1/Q`-ADU under addition modulo Q², hence AU. Signed NHS has bound `2/Q`. Concatenating t Toeplitz-shifted NH outputs, with an even two-word key shift, gives `Q^-t` AU (and the same argument permits fixed additive targets). Its output has **2wt bits**, not wt bits.

RFC UHASH produces 32t bits for `t∈{1,2,3,4}` using 1024-byte unsigned-NH blocks, a two-prime polynomial layer, and a prime-field inner-product/truncation layer. RFC §6.2 states ideal MAC forgery bounds `2^-30t`, plus an AES term in deployment. The 1999 paper's proof is not a proof of this later three-layer algorithm; the 2000 dissertation still uses signed NH, different block sizes/parameters, and proves its own variant. A standardized UHASH collision or differential theorem must be connected to the exact RFC key domains and stages, not copied from an NH theorem or a MAC headline.

**2. NH proof, with the delicate integer step explicit.**

1. Pick a differing word, place it in the second coordinate of one pair, and condition on all key words except that pair's first key. Then equality at any fixed additive target reduces to `c((k+m) mod Q)−c'((k+m') mod Q)=v mod Q²`, with distinct `c,c'∈[0,Q)`.
2. Translate k to make m=0. As k crosses `Q−m'`, the other operand wraps once. In each interval the equation is linear with slope `c−c'`. It has at most one solution in `[0,Q)`: two would imply `(c−c')(k−k')` is a nonzero multiple of Q², although its absolute value is strictly less than Q².
3. There cannot be one solution on each side of the wrap either. If `k1<Q−m'≤k2`, subtraction forces `(k2−k1)(c'−c)=Qc' mod Q²`. When `c'>c`, both sides are nonnegative and below Q², while the left is strictly smaller than Qc'. When `c'<c`, changing sign would require `(k2−k1)(c−c')=Q²−Qc'`, again larger than the possible left side. Thus there is at most **one total** key value, not one per interval, proving `1/Q`.
4. The bound is attained when one message coordinate changes and its unchanged partner is keyed to zero. For signed NHS the cross-interval exclusion does not hold; there can be two solutions, giving `2/Q`. Replacing the word additions by XOR is a different family and this proof does not apply.
5. For Toeplitz NH, remove common suffix pairs and select the last differing pair. In repetition j, the two new key positions beyond those used in repetitions `<j` remain uniform. Fix one of them and all earlier keys. The same one-solution argument applies to the other. Consequently `Pr[E_j | E_1,…,E_(j−1)]≤1/Q`; multiplying these **conditional** probabilities gives `Q^-t` despite key overlap.
6. The unequal-length extension asserted in the 2007 VHASH note is false for **unmodified NH**: compare a zero pair with two zero pairs. Their difference is the extra keyed product, zero with probability `2/Q−1/Q²>1/Q`. This is not fixed by excluding empty messages. Length encoding must actually be part of the analyzed function.

**2a. What can be certified directly for RFC UHASH.** A conservative bound for **one full 32-bit component** can be derived without claiming the unverified `t`-fold theorem. Assume full IID random KDF bytes, independent between L1, L2 and L3. Let `p36=2^36−5` and set

`E_U = 2^-32 + 2^-35 + (2^54+6)/2^100 + 32(2^28+1)/2^64`.

Here is the derivation, including the version-dependent constants.

1. L1 adds the actual chunk bit length to NH. Same pair counts use NH's ADU result. For unequal pair counts, start with the common prefix difference and target equal to the nonzero length difference. If prefixes agree, initial probability is zero; otherwise it is at most `2^-32`. Add extra products one at a time. With Q=2^32, a nonzero first factor gives at most one second factor (their possible product differences have magnitude below Q²); a zero first factor leaves the previous event. Thus `p_new≤(1−1/Q)/Q+p_old/Q≤1/Q`. If the number of L1 chunks differs, the output vectors have different lengths. Hence L1 vector AU is at most `2^-32`.
2. RFC POLY begins with 1, and escapes large data words by marker `p−1` followed by the word minus the field offset. The marker is outside the unescaped range; escape payloads and unescaped words are below p. Parsing is injective, and the leading 1 separates encoded lengths. Each input word becomes at most two field symbols.
3. The 64-bit polynomial key has 50 random bits. The first-stage cap is `2^14` 64-bit input words, so its degree is at most `2^15`, giving `2^-35`. For source messages `<2^64` bytes, the L1 output **per component** is at most `2^57` bytes. The 128-bit layer has at most `2^53+3` input words after prefix result and terminator padding, hence degree at most `2^54+6`, on a 100-bit key set. Add the displayed root bound. A short/long L2 comparison can be treated as the long nonconstant polynomial minus a fixed short result. A short message bypasses L2 with a zero-high-half representation; the leading polynomial term still prevents an identically zero cross-branch polynomial.
4. For distinct 16-byte L3 inputs, at least one 16-bit coordinate differs modulo p36. Conditioning on the other coefficients, the inner-product difference is a nonzero slope times one key coefficient. A coefficient is a uniform 64-bit integer reduced modulo p36, with point mass at most `ceil(2^64/p36)/2^64=(2^28+1)/2^64`.
5. Equality of the low 32 bits requires the two canonical prime residues to differ by a multiple of `2^32`. Their integer difference is between `−(p36−1)` and `p36−1`; fewer than 32 such lifts are possible, so using **32** is a safe upper bound. Each fixed lift is covered by the preceding differential bound. The final XOR translation does not affect equality. Independent composition adds the four terms in E_U.
6. Equality of a complete 64-, 96-, or 128-bit UHASH output implies equality of its first 32-bit component. Therefore E_U is also a valid, deliberately weak bound for each complete output. Squaring or taking higher powers requires an additional analysis of the shifted L1 keys and conditional events; it has **not** been silently assumed here.

The dissertation's fuller joint analysis is evidence for its own version, but is not a checked proof of all the RFC changes. AES expansion and a nonce pad also change the question: equality under a common pad preserves hash equality; independent nonce pads produce a different event. The stated MAC forgery bound includes an adversarial game, nonce restrictions and a computational term; it is not an unconditional theorem about a 128-bit expanded seed family.

**3. Enumeration.** NH with w=2: all 16 messages ×16 keys gives max AU **1/4**, and max over every additive δ is also **1/4**. Two Toeplitz outputs give **1/16** over all 256 one-pair keys. A separate test with **two message pairs, 256 messages, and 4096 keys**, where the two outputs share their middle pair of key words, also gives **1/16**. The unequal-length zero-extension pair gives **7/16**, refuting 1/4. These check the foundational bounds, including actual Toeplitz key overlap; they do not verify the RFC's complete multi-output theorem.

**4. Score and verdict.** NH32's smallest pair is one 8-byte word: **32 bits at L=1**. NH64's literal pair domain starts at two 8-byte words: **65 bits at L=2**; allowing one data word plus fixed zero padding yields **64 at L=1**. More generally score `wt+log2(L_min)` for a constant Toeplitz bound. The RFC's claimed constants would score **30t at L=1**. The verified length-independent conservative E_U gives **`−log2 E_U≈30.3561` at L=1**, for any output containing that component. In particular, **E_U<2^-30 verifies the 32-bit hash collision claim** by this independent derivation. It does not prove the MAC's differential/forgery theorem. **NH and Toeplitz NH hold; the advertised `2^-60`, `2^-90`, and `2^-120` standardized UHASH collision bounds could not be verified from the located primary proofs.**

## 6. VHASH and VMAC

**Primary sources:** Dai–Krovetz, [VHASH Security, 2007/338](https://eprint.iacr.org/2007/338), Theorem 1, Corollary 2, Lemmas 3–4 and Theorem 6; [saved full text](gathered/vmac-nh-umac/vhash-security-eprint-2007-338.txt). [VMAC draft specification](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-krovetz-vmac-01). The 2006 SAC paper describes an earlier final modulus and weaker bound; its constants must not be substituted for the revised algorithm.

**1. Precise statement.** Let `b=1024` bits, positive `N=ceil(max_bytes/128)` and `r=2^64−257`. A message has `n=max(1,ceil(bytes/128))` blocks; its last block is zero padded to a multiple of 16 bytes. For the empty message, n=1 and its empty NH sum is zero. VHASH uses a uniform b-bit NH key; a uniform polynomial key from the **2^116-element** set whose four 32-bit limbs are each below `2^29`; and independent uniform `k1,k2∈[0,r)`. NH results are reduced modulo `2^126`, combined modulo `P=2^127−1` as

`k^n + a1 k^(n−1)+…+an + last_bitlength·2^64`,

and split by integer quotient/remainder base `2^64−2^32`. Here `last_bitlength` is the actual pre-padding last-block length, including 1024 for a full block and 0 for the empty message. The final output is `(p1+k1)(p2+k2) mod r`, a full canonical residue in `[0,r)` represented in 64 bits.

Theorem 1 gives ADU in addition modulo r:

`e_V(N)=2^-62 + 1/r + N·2^-115`.

Corollary 2 gives ADU in addition modulo `2^64` with **`2 e_V(N)`**. Equality of the actual outputs is already covered by Theorem 1; changing the difference group does not require doubling the **zero-difference** bound. The `2^-61` headline uses messages of at most `2^62` bits and the r-additive theorem, not the larger corollary.

**2. Re-derivation, including a repair.**

1. Equal-length NH is `2^-64`-ADU modulo `2^128`. Reducing to `2^126` collects exactly four residue classes of the difference, giving `2^-62`-ADU. This factor is justified because the moduli divide; it is not an arbitrary “entropy loss” argument.
2. The note's assertion that unmodified NH has the same bound at different pair counts is false (§5). Its **truncated** NH bound needed here can nevertheless be repaired. Condition on every key except one extra product in the longer message. For Q=2^64 and modulus `Q²/4`, a nonzero first factor other than Q/2 permits at most one second factor in `[0,Q)`; Q/2 permits at most two. For a nonzero target, zero first factor permits none, so there are at most Q solutions. For target zero, direct valuation counting gives exactly 2Q solutions: `a=0` or `b=0`, plus `a=b=Q/2`. Thus the maximum point probability is `2/Q≤4/Q=2^-62`. Adding a fixed offset and conditioning on earlier products preserves this bound. This proves the unequal-pair-count fact actually needed after truncation.
3. The coefficient encoding is nonzero with high probability. Different block counts have different leading monic powers `k^n`, so the difference polynomial is nonzero for every block key. At equal block counts, a differing nonlast padded block supplies a nonzero coefficient except with probability ≤`2^-62`. If only the last block/length differs, its coefficient is `an+last_bitlength·2^64 mod P`. For unequal padded contents, the required equality fixes at most one ordinary difference of the an values modulo P, because their difference range has width `<P`; use truncated NH's additive-target bound. Equal padded contents with distinct lengths give a nonzero deterministic length term. For b=1024 that term cannot wrap P.
4. Condition on a nonzero coefficient polynomial. Its degree is at most N and the evaluation set has 2^116 points, so collision probability is at most `N/2^116`. This direct equality argument is already enough for final ADU composition, and is stronger than the note's looser `N/2^115` term. It supplies a clean repair without the note's abbreviated prefix/suffix expression, which suppresses the leading monic and length terms.
5. Quotient/remainder splitting is injective and both resulting coordinates lie below r: the remainder is below `2^64−2^32<r`, and the quotient is approximately 2^63, also below r. For two different pairs, expand the final-product difference. At least one nonzero coefficient multiplies a fresh uniform field key, giving **exactly `1/r`** for any fixed additive target. Add the preceding AU bound, obtaining Theorem 1's advertised, slightly looser formula.
6. To move to differences modulo `2^64`, the canonical output difference lies in `−(r−1)…r−1`. A fixed `2^64` residue has at most two lifts there. Each is covered by the r-additive differential bound, giving Corollary 2. For equality, only lift 0 is possible, so no doubling is needed.
7. For two **fully independently keyed** VHASH iterations, full 128-bit equality is at most `e_V(N)^2`. The deployed VMAC draft shifts/reuses NH key material. A power claim for that version needs the Toeplitz/conditional argument with its complete polynomial and length processing; independence cannot merely be asserted. AES-derived keys and nonce masking add computational/game assumptions. The VMAC `2^-60` / `2^-120` forgery headlines describe that MAC, not the exact VHASH output event above.

**3. Enumeration.** The truncation and polynomial components reduce to the NH/root checks above. The final NMH map over F5 has max ADU **1/5**, across all 25 input pairs, 25 keys, and all additive targets. The false pre-truncation unequal-length lemma is explicitly refuted by 7/16 in the w=2 test. The 64-bit theorem is certified by the repaired proof, not by a toy full implementation with mismatched length encodings.

**4. Score and verdict.** Use `N=ceil(L/16)`. The minimum of `L/(a+c ceil(L/16))`, for positive a,c, is at **L=1**: on each plateau it increases, and at successive plateau starts it also increases. The full-output collision bound scores **61.6780719**; the doubled ADU formula scores **60.6780719**. Two fully independent iterations score **123.3561438 at L=1** on the stated `L≤2^56`-word domain. Indeed N≤2^52, so `cN≤2^-63<a/10` for `a=2^-62+1/r`, `c=2^-115`. For L≥2, `L/(a+cN)^2≥2/(1.1a)^2>1/a²>1/(a+c)^2`. **Theorem 1's bound holds with the repaired intermediate argument.** The original unmodified-NH unequal-length assertion does not.

## 7. HalftimeHash

**Primary sources:** Jim Apple, [HalftimeHash, arXiv:2104.08865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08865), generalized EHC theorem and cumulative analysis; [current TeX](audit-support/halftime-current.tex), [current implementation](https://github.com/jbapple/HalftimeHash/blob/main/halftime-hash.hpp), [downloaded header](audit-support/halftime-current.hpp). The numbers 64/128/256/512 in the public API describe styles/SIMD width, **not output bits**: all four public wrappers return 64 bits. HalftimeHash24 is the paper's 192-bit core variant.

**1. Claimed and corrected statements.** For the abstract core with k 64-bit outputs, the paper claims AU on equal-length strings with

`ε_claim=2^(-32k)(2^(kp)+h^k+1)`.

The parameter list defines p as the **largest power of two dividing a relevant determinant** and explicitly sets p=4 for HalftimeHash24. Read literally, its formula is therefore **`2^-96(4096+h³+1)`**. The proof instead computes with the 2-adic **valuation**, whose maximum is a=2 here. Using that sharper EHC calculation would give **`2^-96(64+h³+1)`** if the later tree argument were valid. The printed divisor-based EHC term is looser, not an invalid upper bound merely because it is larger. The paper's assertion about how doubling p changes ε does not agree with its displayed formula.

For HalftimeHash24, k=3 and the intended physical leaf size is 168 64-bit words (1344 bytes). Append the length to the output to separate unequal lengths; that changes the output format and does not prove a fixed-width folded API bound.

A conservative bound provable by ordinary composition for the **abstract construction with a correct distance-3 encoder** is

`ε_safe(L)=2^-96` for `L<168`, and
`ε_safe(L)=2^-90+(h(L)+1)2^-32` for `L≥168`,

where `h(L)=max(0,floor(log_8(floor(L/168))))` is the actual completed-tree height. This does not claim the paper's much stronger bound. The scalar/simd 64-bit wrappers require their own analysis, including the key layout and finite stack.

**2. Re-derivation and errors.**

1. A systematic distance-k erasure code ensures at least k differing encoded symbols. For the intended k=3 encoder, the two parity symbols are `Σv_i` and `ΣA_i v_i` on three-component bit vectors. The seven 3×3 binary maps A_i and all 21 pairwise differences are invertible (checked exhaustively). One changed data symbol therefore changes both parities; with two changed symbols either the first parity changes, or their equal differences force the second parity to change. Three or more changed data symbols already give distance at least 3. This proves the intended abstract code property, separately from the faulty current `Encode3` implementation below. Fix k such locations and condition on the keys at the others. Independent NH at these locations supplies independent differences, each with point probability at most `2^-32` under integer subtraction modulo `2^64`. With b parallel interleaved instances sharing entropy, whole-leaf equality implies equality of any one fixed changed instance; independence between SIMD lanes is not required.
2. If A is the k×k selected submatrix of the combine matrix T, collision at a fixed additive vector target requires `AΔ=δ mod 2^64`. Write `det A=u2^a'` with u odd and `a'≤a`. Multiply by the adjugate. A necessary condition is `2^a' Δ=u^-1 adj(A)δ`. If the right side is not divisible coordinatewise by `2^a'`, probability is zero. Otherwise divide it by that power and reduce modulo `2^(64−a')`. Each coordinate permits `2^a'` original 64-bit differences. Their union bound is `2^a'2^-32`; independence across the k NH keys gives `2^(k(a'−32))≤2^(k(a−32))`.
3. The final probability equalities in the paper's chain must be **≤**, and its β after division must include that division. Enumerating all 84 three-column minors of the displayed 3×9 matrix finds no zero determinant and maximum valuation **2**, establishing the sharpened constant 64 in the k=3 EHC term. The literal p=4 bound uses 4096 instead, which remains a valid but looser EHC constant because `a≤2^a`. The adjugate argument is valid with these distinctions and the missing division repaired.
4. A single h-level tree is `h·2^-32`-AU. Induct on height: if the selected differing child subtree collides, pay the previous bound; otherwise condition on child results and use the independent next-level key. No union over all nodes is necessary. The tree's NH variant leaves the last 64-bit input unhashed: if only it changes, equality is impossible; otherwise the differing hashed prefix is covered by NH's ADU bound at the fixed difference contributed by that last input. This justifies the same node constant for that variant.
5. The paper then raises this tree bound to the kth power **conditional only on noncollision of the EHC vector**. That is invalid: distinct k-vectors can agree in k−1 coordinates. Those coordinate trees then have identical input, and their equality probability is 1, not `h·2^-32`. Independent tree keys do not make identical inputs distinct. The paper's earlier prose also states `h ε^k`, inconsistent with the later `(hε)^k`. Neither resolves the missing coordinate-distinctness argument. A proof would have to track partial EHC collisions and their probabilities jointly with the tree events.
6. A safe repair is to treat all k trees as a single vector-valued map. Distinct input vectors differ in at least one component, so its AU bound is `h·2^-32`. The final NH compression of a differing component costs at most another `2^-32`. Add EHC's `2^-90`, yielding the safe formula. If the raw tail differs, its fresh Toeplitz-NH keys provide `2^-96` at any fixed vector difference, so tail cancellation does not enlarge that envelope. With no EHC leaves, the entire message is handled by this tail hash and has bound `2^-96`.
7. The published height formula mixes a stated 32-bit-word input convention with b defined as a number of 64-bit words, while the entropy helper takes a byte argument without the corresponding conversion. At short lengths its logarithm is undefined. The safe formula above uses **physical bytes divided by the actual leaf size**, and explicitly sets h=0 where appropriate. A collision probability at one megabyte/exabyte is not the paper's length-normalized score.
8. **Concrete current-code failure for the 24-byte variant:** `Encode3` captures `iter` by value in `DistributeRaw`; subsequent changes to the outer pointer do not change which data that lambda reads. Its pointer increments also advance individual Blocks, not rows of three. A pair of all-zero inputs differing only at `raw_io[6]` produces encoded arrays differing in **one** of the nine symbols. Distance 3, needed by the theorem, is false for that function. The test was compiled directly from the downloaded function: [source](audit-support/halftime-encode3-check.cpp), [output](audit-support/halftime-encode3-check.log). The current public wrappers instantiate k=2/`Encode2`, so this is not a claim that those wrappers execute `Encode3`.
9. The proposed 64-bit wrapper bound in the gathered record adds `2^-64` for an independent simple-tabulation finalizer. In `TabulateAfter`, however, the core starts at `entropy + width*256` 64-bit words, while tabulation covers `(width+1)*8*256` words. At width=2 the core begins at word **512** and the tabulation region extends to **6144**. In particular, the **length tables overlap core key material**. For equal lengths, that common length-table term cancels; output-table regions may still be independent of the core on bounded inputs. But this is not the general independent-composition argument used for the gathered all-length bound. The declared table reference also has only `1+width` rows although later indexing uses rows 8 and 16. Finally `Hash` allocates only nine stack levels with no unrestricted-input proof. One cannot certify arbitrary byte lengths from the exabyte formula and this implementation.
10. PRNG-populated entropy (MT19937, splitmix/Romu in wrappers) is another, separate gap from the ideal random table model.

**3. Enumeration.** The 84 minor determinants give a=2. A complete tiny distance-2 EHC instance (16 messages, 64 independent keys, 1-bit NH operands) attains **1/4**, matching `(2^-1)^2`. The tree-conditioning counterexample uses input vectors `(0,0)` and `(1,0)` with independent uniform affine maps over F4: collision probability is **1/4**, not 1/16. It refutes the stated conditional step, not by itself the finished hash formula. The exact current `Encode3` witness has measured symbol distance **1**, as above.

**4. Score and verdict.** For the corrected abstract k=3 envelope, lengths below 168 score `96+log2 L`; at L=168 the bound switches to `2^-90+2^-32`, giving

`32+log2 168−log2(1+2^-58) ≈ 39.3923174`.

At subsequent height changes, L increases by a factor 8 while h+1 grows by at most a factor 2; within a height interval the score increases. Thus **L=168 is the global minimum** of this conservative bound. It is not an estimate of the actual worst pair.

If one merely extends the **unverified printed** cumulative formula to h=0 at L=1, it scores **`96−log2 4097≈83.9996478` at L=1**. The valuation-sharpened version would score **`96−log2 65≈89.9776322` at L=1**. Those minima follow because L grows by a factor 8 at height changes while `4097+h³` or `65+h³` grows by less than that factor. The paper's “more than 83 bits” is a pointwise collision-entropy claim below one exabyte, not the normalized score. In fact h=16 occurs below that cap, and its literal formula gives `8193/2^96>2^-83`: even that strict numerical phrase needs correction.

Similarly the proposed 64-bit `2^-64(18+h²)` formula gives **`64−log2 18=59.8301` at L=1** under the h=0 short-input extension. These are calculations on claims, not certificates. **The advertised strong bound could not be verified; a weaker abstract bound holds with correction. The current 24-byte code additionally fails its encoding assumption.**

## 8. Multiply-shift and pair-multiply-shift

**Primary sources:** Dietzfelbinger–Hagerup–Katajainen–Penttonen, [A Reliable Randomized Algorithm for the Closest-Pair Problem](https://hjemmesider.diku.dk/~jyrki/Paper/CP-11.4.1997.pdf), Lemma 2.4, [downloaded PDF](audit-support/dietzfelbinger-1997.pdf); Thorup, [High Speed Hashing for Integers and Strings, arXiv:1504.06804](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06804), §§2.3, 3.3–3.5, 5.1, Theorems 3.5/3.7, exercises 3.10/5.1; [saved text](gathered/multiply-shift-tabulation/thorup_1504.06804.txt). Dietzfelbinger's STACS 1996 chapter is the origin of the integer-arithmetic strong-universality construction, but the full proof used here is Thorup's primary technical exposition. `1505.01523` is the tabulation survey, **not** these numbered multiply-shift results.

**1. Precise families.**

- Odd one-word version: `h_a(x)=high_r(a x mod 2^w)`, `x∈[0,2^w)`, a uniform among odd w-bit integers. Bound `min(1,2/2^r)` AU, generally not pairwise independent; `h(0)=0`.
- Vector version: `h(x)=high_r((b+Σ a_i x_i) mod 2^W)`, `x_i∈[0,2^w)`, all a_i and b independent uniform W-bit integers, **`W≥w+r−1`**. Exact pairwise independence, hence full-output collision **`2^-r`**.
- Pair version on even d: replace the sum by `Σ(a_(2i)+x_(2i+1))(a_(2i+1)+x_(2i))`, keeping the same W-bit arithmetic and independent b. Same theorem. Products are not NH products with additions reduced prematurely modulo `2^w`.
- Prefix version: for even d≤D, replace b by independent length-indexed `a_d` unused by that prefix. A final partial word needs an injective byte encoding, not arbitrary zero padding across lengths.

**2. Proof.**

1. Odd version: write the nonzero input difference as `2^s z` with z odd. Multiplication by z permutes odd residues. The product difference has s low zero bits, bit s equal to one, and uniform bits above s. If `s≥w−r`, the outputs cannot be equal: the product difference is a nonzero multiple of the bucket width. Otherwise equality of high bits requires the high r bits of the difference to be all zero or all one, depending on a carry from low bits. Each event has probability `2^-r`, so their union is at most `2^(1−r)`. Allowing arbitrary even multipliers destroys the bound: inputs differing only in their top bit collide for all even a.
2. Vector version: choose s to be the minimum 2-adic valuation among nonzero coordinate differences. Condition on all multipliers except one attaining s. Then `g(y)−g(x)=2^s(z a_j+c)` with z odd; the high `W−s` bits are uniform. The low s bits are zero. Conditional on all multipliers, independent b makes `g(x)+b` uniform over `2^W`. Therefore it is independent of the uniform difference. On the high `W−s` bits, the two values are independent uniform. Since `s≤w−1≤W−r`, the r selected bits are within that range, proving exact pairwise independence.
3. Pair version: expand the product difference. The key-key product cancels, leaving the same linear-in-key difference as the vector version plus `Σ(y_even y_odd−x_even x_odd)`. Each summand of that extra constant is divisible by `2^s`, because every coordinate difference is. The same odd-slope argument therefore applies, and fresh b supplies the uniform translation. This fills in Thorup's exercise rather than citing it as an already-written proof.
4. Prefix version: equal lengths use step 3. For d<d', the longer prefix's `a_d'` is absent from both the shorter hash and the longer products, so it makes the longer result uniform independently of the shorter. The shorter is itself uniform using its own length offset. The fact that the shorter offset occurs in a longer product does not matter after conditioning on it. Two fresh length cases are not required.
5. Output truncation to the specified high bits is already in the theorem; taking arbitrary low bits instead is not. Omitting b gives only the weaker AU guarantee. Choosing odd multipliers in a same-width vector dot product is not the same construction: two top-bit coordinate changes can cancel deterministically.
6. **Paper/code qualifications:** the saved `VectorMultShift` has **65×128-bit keys**, i.e. **130 64-bit words** at its 64-word limit. The table's `L+1` count, explicitly measured in 64-bit words, should be **`2(L+1)`** for that implementation. It reads only complete 8-byte words; arbitrary leftover bytes are ignored, so its byte-string domain must be restricted to multiples of 8. It is capped at 64 input words. Its PRNG initialization is not a proof of independent uniform keys.
7. The saved SMHasher3 multiply-shift variants cycle key indices modulo 256. Once positions repeat, swapping corresponding data words/pairs yields deterministic collisions under the linear/pair sum; a length term cannot distinguish a same-length swap. They also do not implement the theorem's fresh independent b in the simple way specified above, and their tables come from a small PRNG seed. A name shared with the mathematical family does not transfer the theorem.

**3. Enumeration.** Odd multiply-shift with w=4,r=2, all 16 inputs and 8 odd keys attains **1/2** (inputs 1 and 3), showing the factor 2 is real. Vector and pair versions with w=2,W=3,r=2 have 16 input vectors and 512 keys: every distinct pair has collision probability **1/4**, and all 16 ordered output cells have exactly **32/512=1/16** probability.

**4. Score and verdict.** The odd scheme's loose bound scores `r−1` at its one-word input (for w=64). When r=w=64 it is actually a permutation: zero collisions, scored **64** by the floor, so do not unnecessarily report 63 for that special case. Vector/pair output of 64 bits scores **64 at L=1** when the encoded one-word domain is admitted; an unpadded two-64-bit-word pair domain starts at L=2 and scores 65. **The mathematical bounds hold; the benchmark domain, key-size count, and seeded/reused-key implementations need the stated corrections.**

## 9. Simple tabulation

**Primary source:** Pătraşcu–Thorup, [The Power of Simple Tabulation Hashing, arXiv:1011.5200](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.5200), 2011 conference / 2012 JACM version, introduction and Appendix A.3; [saved primary text](gathered/multiply-shift-tabulation/patrascu_thorup_1011.5200.txt).

**1. Statement.** For a fixed c-character universe `Σ^c`, let every entry of every position-specific table `T_i:Σ→{0,1}^r` be independent uniform. Then `H(x)=xor_i T_i[x_i]` is exactly 3-independent, hence AU and AXU with ε=`2^-r`. The random key is the entire collection of entries, not a PRNG seed that fills it.

**2. Proof and limitations.** For two different keys choose a differing position. Its entry for one key is absent from the other; condition on all remaining entries. That fresh uniform entry makes the XOR difference uniform. For three different keys, some position has a character unique to one of the three: otherwise at every position either all agree, or a nonconstant multiset of three would have to contain no singleton, which is impossible. Condition on tables not including that singleton entry. The selected hash is fresh uniform; the two others remain pairwise independent when averaging the remaining tables. Inducting from one to two to three outputs proves exact 3-independence. Four keys at the corners of a two-character rectangle have XOR of all four hashes identically zero, so 4-independence fails.

Variable-length strings must be embedded injectively with position/length markers, or first compressed with another analyzed family. For independent universe reduction F and tabulation T, the composed pair bound is `e_F+(1−e_F)2^-r`, not `2^-r`. Three-independence conditional on distinct signatures is not unconditional independence of the original strings. The saved SMHasher3 “tabulation” implementation includes multiply-shift and Mersenne polynomial compression, and splitmix-generated tables; its full-string guarantee is not proved by Pătraşcu–Thorup's theorem.

For completeness, the **twisted tabulation** named in the same gathered row is also exactly 3-independent under its ideal key model. In Thorup's [author exposition, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01523), choose a simple-tabulation twister `t:Σ^(c−1)→Σ` and independent simple-tabulation output tables S. Map `(x0,tail)` to `(x0 xor t(tail),tail)`, then apply S. For every fixed t, this preprocessing is a permutation: the unchanged tail determines the XOR needed to recover x0. Distinct triples remain distinct, and independent S supplies the already-proved uniform triple distribution conditional on t. Averaging proves the assertion, hence the same `2^-r` collision bound and score. No concentration claim is needed for this audit. The separate random bits in each combined twister/output table entry must be independent; a small seeded table generator does not inherit this theorem.

**3. Enumeration.** Two binary characters and 2-bit table entries: all 256 simple tables give pair collision **1/4** for every pair; all 64 possible output triples occur four times for each triple of distinct keys. The four-corner XOR is zero for every table. Adding all four possible twisters gives 1024 twisted keys; every distinct triple again has a uniform output distribution, and max collision remains **1/4**.

**4. Score and verdict.** For the 64-bit one-word universe the score is **64 at L=1**. For a single fixed c-character universe occupying `L0` words, the score is instead `64+log2 L0`; minimizing over lengths means comparing a consistently parameterized set of families. **The pure simple-tabulation bound holds. The full composite/seeded implementation needs a separate bound.**

## 10. Carter–Wegman and polynomial hashing over prime/Mersenne fields

**Primary sources:** Carter–Wegman, [Universal Classes of Hash Functions, 1979](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/7/36891/files/2020/10/CarterWegmanJrCompSci1979UniversalHashClasses.pdf), Lemma 6 and Proposition 7, [downloaded scan](audit-support/carter-wegman-1979.pdf); Bernstein, [Polynomial evaluation and message authentication](https://cr.yp.to/papers.html#pema), 2007-10-22, §4.2, [saved full text](gathered/poly-mersenne-brw-horner/pema-20071022.txt). The 1979 affine-hashing theorem, the 1981 independence paper, and fixed-message polynomial evaluation are related but are not the same theorem. The VHASH reference list interchanges the 1979/1981 titles and dates; use the actual documents.

**1. Precise statements.**

- Carter–Wegman affine family: prime p, inputs `x∈[0,p)`, independent uniform `a∈F_p*`, `b∈F_p`, and a balanced map `g:F_p→[0,B)`, with `B≤p`. Full output `g(ax+b mod p)` is `1/B`-AU. Before g, the affine maps are permutations, so distinct inputs cannot collide. Allowing a=0 and retaining the full residue instead gives exact pairwise independence with collision `1/p`.
- Fixed-length unshifted Horner: `H_x(m)=Σ_(i=1)^d m_i x^(d−i)` with `m_i∈F_p`, uniform `x∈F_p`, full canonical output. ε=`min(1,(d−1)/p)`. This is AU, **not general ADU**: changing only the last coefficient gives a constant difference.
- Positive-power version: `Σ m_i x^(d+1−i)`. Its difference for distinct fixed-length messages has a nonzero positive-degree coefficient even after subtracting any constant δ. Thus ε=`min(1,d/p)`-ADU. An independent uniform field pad makes output pairs have joint probability at most `d/p²`.
- Variable length requires an injective encoding, such as a leading monic marker and an unambiguous chunk/length representation. Merely prepending zeros is not enough. Every coefficient must be representable in the chosen field without collisions.

**2. Re-derivation and implementation qualifications.**

1. For affine hashing and fixed x≠y, every ordered pair u≠v of field outputs has exactly one solution `a=(u−v)/(x−y)`, `b=u−ax`, and a is nonzero. Therefore `(u,v)` is uniform over `p(p−1)` ordered distinct pairs. If fibre sizes of g are n_j, exact collision probability is `Σ n_j(n_j−1)/(p(p−1))`. Since `n_j−1≤ceil(p/B)−1≤(p−1)/B`, summing gives at most `1/B`. This is the actual 1979 proof; assuming p is an arbitrary modulus or allowing input x=p would break it.
2. For Horner, coefficientwise injectivity gives a nonzero difference polynomial of degree ≤d−1. Root counting gives the AU statement. In the positive-power version a nonzero data coefficient occurs at a positive power, so subtracting δ cannot erase the polynomial; degree ≤d proves ADU. An independent pad supplies one of p possible pad values for every selected evaluation key, giving the joint bound. This is the precise condition missing from Polymur's arbitrary-permutation argument.
3. For Mersenne p=`2^b−1`, write an integer product as `u2^b+v`. It is congruent to u+v modulo p because `2^b≡1`; repeat the fold and perform canonical correction as required by the operand bound. This is an implementation of a prime field only if p is actually prime and no machine overflow discards a nonmultiple of p. A Mersenne-shaped modulus is not automatically prime.
4. A 64-bit input word cannot be reduced blindly modulo `2^61−1`: words 0 and p become identical for **every key**. Use an alphabet inside the field (e.g. 32- or 56-bit chunks with length encoding). Conversely p=`2^89−1` can contain a full 64-bit coefficient injectively, but its full output occupies 89 bits.
5. **Truncation is not free for AU.** Let `B=2^64`, `p=2^89−1`, and compare field polynomials x and `x+B−1`, with x uniform over F_p. Their full residues never collide. Their low 64 bits coincide exactly when the second value wraps modulo p, for **`(B−1)/p≈2^-25`** of the keys. This is because `p≡−1 mod B`. Replacing B by `2^32` and p by `2^61−1` gives approximately `2^-29`. A lift-root proof fails here because one shifted difference polynomial is identically zero. This is why the unshifted Horner AU theorem cannot simply be multiplied by a truncation factor.
6. The SMHasher3 `poly-mersenne.deg0…deg4` implementation uses 32-bit characters, a leading byte length, evaluation keys in `[0,2^60−1)` (size **2^60−1**), and only 32 output bits. For uniform evaluation on that set and an injective length encoding, its full-residue bound is at most `ceil(bytes/4)/(2^60−1)`. A leading length must itself remain unambiguous modulo p; a range such as byte length `<p` is needed for the simple argument. The code's `len/p` comment has the wrong key denominator and is not a proof of the truncated output.
7. For degree K≥1 finalization with **independent uniform field coefficients**, two distinct residue inputs give independent uniform field outputs: the two evaluation rows have rank 2. If `p=B r−1`, `B=2^32`, `r=2^29`, truncating those two independent residues has exact collision probability
   `e_T=((B−1)r²+(r−1)²)/p² = 1/B+(B−1)/(B p²)`.
   If the first stage's collision bound is e, the complete bound is `e+(1−e)e_T`. This is a justified projection argument because the conditional joint distribution has been proved. It is not applicable to the no-finalizer `deg0` case. Nor does a K-degree finalizer make the original string hash `(K+1)`-independent when first-stage signatures can collide.
8. The actual coefficients/evaluation key are produced by splitmix-derived `rand_u128` and remainder operations, not uniform independent field samples. Modulo sampling also has a finite bias unless rejection sampling is used. Thus the ideal corrected calculation is not an information-theoretic guarantee for the shipped seed family. The [Ahle–Knudsen–Thorup Mersenne paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08654) does not supply a missing theorem for this particular variable-string wrapper.

**3. Enumeration.** For affine hashing with p=7 and B=4, all 42 keys and all input pairs give exact max **1/7≤1/4**. Unshifted three-symbol Horner over F5 attains **2/5** across all 125 messages and 5 keys. These are complete maxima. The positive-power, marked-coefficient toy over p=61 in §15 separately checks differential lift counting for a truncated field output; that is a different theorem from unshifted Horner AU.

**4. Score and verdict.** Normalization must be fixed before assigning bits:

- The affine family with B output values and one-word inputs scores **`log2 B` at L=1** from its published `1/B` bound. Restrict the input universe to values below p. A full-residue affine permutation has zero collisions and scores its output width under the paper's floor.
- With **one injectively represented coefficient per 8-byte slot** (restrict words to values below p when `p<2^64`), full `b`-bit Mersenne output and the paper's floor, unshifted Horner scores **b at L=1**. For p=`2^b−1`, for `2≤L≤p`, `L/((L−1)/p)≥p+1=2^b`, and for `L≥p+1`, clipping at 1 gives at least `2^b`. This proves the global minimum, not an informal large-L limit. Positive-power Horner scores `log2 p` using the `L/p` bound.
- For **32-bit byte chunks**, two symbols occupy each 8-byte word. The fixed-length full-residue Horner bound is `(2L−1)/p`, whose score for p=`2^61−1` is exactly **60 at `L=2^60`**, where the capped bound reaches 1. Before that, `L/(2L−1)` decreases; afterward L itself increases. A smaller finite domain is minimized at its maximum L. The restricted-key, length-prefixed signature bound `2L/(2^60−1)` scores **`log2(2^60−1)−1≈59`**, attained at **every admitted L including 1** before saturation. Its simple injective length-domain assumption `8L<p` keeps this bound below saturation.
- Under the ideal degree≥1 finalizer model just specified, substituting `e=2L/(2^60−1)` gives about **31.99999999 bits at L=1**, not a 61-bit score for the shipped 32-bit output. Indeed `L/(e_T+(1−e_T)2L/(2^60−1))` increases with L: divide its denominator by L to see its only variable term `e_T/L` decrease. The actual seeded implementation remains unverified. No certified general score is assigned to `deg0`.

**The field root bounds hold with explicit domains. A prime-field theorem must not be attached to arbitrary 64-bit coefficients, restricted/nonuniform evaluation keys, or truncated AU outputs without the corresponding correction.**

## 11. Horner over GF(2^64)

**Primary sources:** Bernstein §4.2 as above; the paper's [injective.tex](paper/injective.tex), comparison with Horner; Lemire–Kaser §§4–5 for the binary-field arithmetic and irreducible modulus.

**1. Statement.** On a fixed length L≥1 of 64-bit words, interpreted bijectively as F=`GF(2^64)` elements, with uniform x∈F, the full output of unshifted Horner has AU bound `(L−1)/2^64`. Sequential, unrolled and correctly scheduled tree evaluation of the **same polynomial** have the same bound. A positive-power form gives L/q AXU; the unshifted form generally does not.

**2. Proof.** Two distinct messages differ in some field coefficient, so subtracting their formal polynomials is nonzero. Its degree is at most L−1. Root counting over all q keys proves the claim, including x=0 among the bad keys rather than omitting it. Binary polynomial arithmetic is field arithmetic only after reduction by an irreducible polynomial. The modulus `X^64+X^4+X^3+X+1` was rechecked independently (§1). A different reduction constant, ring multiply-fold operation, or unproved truncation is not an equivalent implementation. For variable lengths, `(a)` and `(0,a)` evaluate identically in unshifted Horner, so the fixed-length bound cannot be reused without a marker/length construction. Powers of x used in unrolling are derived powers, not additional independent keys; this is correct because they evaluate the same formal polynomial.

**3. Enumeration.** In GF4, all 64 length-3 messages and all four keys give exact max **1/2=(L−1)/q**. This also tests characteristic-2 reduction.

**4. Score and verdict.** With the paper's floor, L=1 has ε=q^-1 even though actual collision probability is zero. It scores **64**, attained there. For larger L, `Lq/(L−1)>q` until clipping, after which L≥q+1. Thus no other length lowers the score. **Without the output floor**, but correctly clipping probabilities at 1, the exact unrestricted minimum is **`log2(q+1)` at L=q+1**: `Lq/(L−1)` decreases up to that point, after which the ratio is L. Under a smaller cap `2≤M≤q`, it is **`log2(qM/(M−1))` at L=M**. For the common byte cap take M=`2^61−1`. The uncapped formal expression has infimum 64 but is no longer a probability bound at large L. **Bound holds as stated for full fixed-length field outputs.**

## 12. BRW (Bernstein–Rabin–Winograd)

**Primary sources:** Bernstein, [Polynomial evaluation and message authentication](https://cr.yp.to/papers.html#pema), §§5.2–5.7, Theorems 5.4–5.6; [full saved text](gathered/poly-mersenne-brw-horner/pema-20071022.txt). The paper's [injective.tex](paper/injective.tex) also describes a related simplified recurrence with base P()=1; its small base cases are not identical to Bernstein's H()=0.

**1. Statement.** Bernstein defines H()=0, H(a)=a, H(a,b)=aX+b, H(a,b,c)=(X+a)(X²+b)+c. For n≥4 and t the largest power of two ≤n,

`H(m)=H(m_1,…,m_(t−1))(X^t+m_t)+H(m_(t+1),…,m_n)`.

For fixed n the map from field messages to formal polynomials is injective; for n≥3 its degree is **`2^(floor(log2 n)+1)−1`**, with leading coefficient 1. Consequently raw evaluation has AU bound `d_B(n)/q`, where `d_B(1)=0`, `d_B(2)=1`, and `d_B(n)=2^(floor(log2 n)+1)−2` for n≥3. Bernstein's **authenticator** `xH_x(m)+s` has degree-of-difference at most `d_B(n)+1`, and the published convenient bound is `(2n−1)/q`, including additive differences. The raw AU and authenticator bounds must be distinguished.

**2. Proof.**

1. The base cases are immediate by reading polynomial coefficients. For n≥4, left H is monic of degree t−1 and right H has degree at most t−1. The product with X^t therefore has unique leading term X^(2t−1); other terms cannot cancel it. This proves the degree claim by induction. The paper's general statement that sums of monic polynomials are monic is false without degree separation; the separation just proved supplies what is actually needed.
2. The coefficients at powers t through 2t−1 determine the entire left H: divide by X^t and discard the lower terms. By induction they determine the left message.
3. After subtracting X^t times that left polynomial, the coefficient of X^(t−1) is `m_t+c`, where c is the known leading coefficient (0 or 1) of the right polynomial for the **known right length**. This determines m_t. Subtract m_t times the left polynomial, then decode the right part inductively. This proves fixed-length injectivity without a probabilistic assumption.
4. Two fixed-length polynomials have the same leading monomial, so it cancels; degree of the difference is at most d_B(n), not the full degree of H. Evaluating at uniform x then gives the root bound. Multiplying by x adds one degree and ensures a nonzero positive-degree term after subtracting any additive target. An independent uniform pad s gives the one-time authenticator interpretation.
5. Across lengths the coefficient c in step 3 is no longer known. Bernstein's variable-length theorem requires an alphabet S with `0∉S` and `S∩(S+1)=∅`, or a separately proved length encoding. It is not a theorem on arbitrary unmarked variable-length field strings. Finite-field functional identities at very large degrees require clipping ε at 1.
6. The paper's P()=1 variant has P(a)=X+a+1 and P(a,b)=(X+a+1)(X²+b)+1: at n=2, its difference can have degree **2**, versus 1 for Bernstein's H. For n≥3 the same degree-separation decoder applies. A proof attached to an implementation must specify which bases it uses. The loose `(2n−1)/q` AU envelope remains valid for both, but is not the sharp raw bound. A one-key BRW hash is not a multi-independent hash family merely because its coefficient map is injective.

**3. Enumeration.** For n=3 over GF8, all 512 messages and 8 evaluation keys give max **1/4=2/8**, exactly the raw degree-of-difference bound. This is stronger than the published loose authenticator bound `5/8` on that instance.

**4. Score and minimizing length.** The displayed 63 is a rounded score; it is not an exact attained value of every formulation.

- Using the loose published `(2L−1)/q` bound, clipping and flooring, over all positive integer L the minimum is at **`L=q/2=2^63`**, with exact score **`63+log2(q/(q−1))`**. Before saturation `L/(2L−1)` decreases; the next length has ε capped at 1 and a larger ratio.
- Under the common finite byte-length cap, `L≤M=2^61−1`, that loose bound is minimized at **L=M**, with score `64+log2(M/(2M−1))`, slightly above 63.
- For the sharper raw staircase bound, each degree plateau is minimized at its starting power of two. With that same cap, the minimum is at **`L=2^60`**, score **`63−log2(1−2^-60)`**. Without the byte cap it is at **`L=2^63`**, score `63−log2(1−2^-63)`. These finite minima are approximately 63.

**The injectivity/root theorem holds; distinguish raw evaluation, shifted authenticator, length domain and base-case variant.**

## 13. The paper's injective three-key recurrence, lanes, and Mersenne row

**Primary source:** [paper/injective.tex](paper/injective.tex), `def:injective:recurrence`, `lem:injective:coeffmap`; [paper/appendix_chainhash.tex](paper/appendix_chainhash.tex), `lem:ph:injective` and `lem:ph:sz`. Code provenance for the 89-bit discrepancy is preserved in [the saved source excerpts](gathered/poly-mersenne-brw-horner/verified_excerpts_pass2.md).

**1. Statement.** Over a finite field F of size q, with independent uniform `u,y,z∈F`, start `P0=z` and iterate

`P_i=a_i+(b_i+y)(P_(i−1)+u)`.

For **fixed n pairs** `(a_i,b_i)∈F²`, distinct messages have full-field-output collision probability at most `min(1,n/q)`. At unequal numbers n,n' of pairs, the valid general root bound is `(max(n,n')+1)/q`. No length-padded arbitrary-byte theorem follows from the fixed-n statement alone.

The single-key specialization `u=x²,y=x³,z=x`, uniform x∈F, is injective as a formal polynomial and has fixed-n bound `(3n−1)/q`. The two-key version `u=x²,z=x` with independent uniform x,y has fixed-n bound `(n+1)/q`. All require full field output and exact arithmetic.

**2. Complete decoder and degree proof.**

1. Define suffix products `g_i(Y)=Π_(j=i)^n(Y+b_j)` and `g_(n+1)=1`. Expanding the recurrence gives
   `P_n=f1(Y)+Z f2(Y)+U f3(Y)`,
   `f1=Σ a_i g_(i+1)`, `f2=g_1`, `f3=Σ g_i`.
   Induction proves this: multiplication by Y+b_i updates all three coefficient polynomials, while the added U contributes the new suffix term and a_i contributes the constant. No ZU or quadratic U/Z term occurs.
2. Both f2 and f3 are monic of degree n, while `deg f1≤n−1`. Therefore the homogeneous degree-(n+1) part of P_n is **`(Z+U)Y^n`**, independent of the data. The wording that P_n itself is “monic in y” is imprecise over `F[U,Z]`; its leading coefficient is Z+U, not 1. The f2/f3 monicity used in the proof is correct.
3. To decode the b sequence, suppose `(G,S)` describes m remaining terms, with `G=Π_(j=1)^m(Y+β_j)` and `S=Σ_(i=1)^m Π_(j=i)^m(Y+β_j)`. Then
   `[Y^(m−1)]G=Σ β_j`, and
   `[Y^(m−2)](S−G)=Σ_(j=2)^m β_j + 1_(m≥3)`.
   The second equation follows from the leading two surviving suffix products; lower-degree ones cannot reach that coefficient. For m=1 read negative-degree coefficients as zero.
4. Thus `β1=[Y^(m−1)]G−[Y^(m−2)](S−G)+1_(m≥3)`. Divide G by the **monic** factor Y+β1, and replace S by S−G using the old G. The resulting pair has the same form with m−1 terms. There is no division by β1, by differences of message values, or by a possibly zero pivot. Repeated or zero b values are permitted. The missing constant at m=2 is already explicitly corrected in the supplied `injective.tex` parenthesis and the appendix's indicator; it is not an unfixed error in this snapshot.
5. Once all suffix products are known, the leading coefficient of f1 is a1. Subtract `a1 g2`, recover a2 from the next coefficient, and repeat. Therefore every parameter vector is recovered uniquely: the formal coefficient map is injective.
6. Equal n differences cancel `(Z+U)Y^n`, leaving a nonzero total-degree-≤n polynomial. Independent uniform keys give n/q by the multivariate root argument stated at the start of this audit. Unequal n leaves the larger leading homogeneous term, so degree is exactly max(n,n')+1. This difference in degrees is important in ChainHash.
7. Under `Y=x³,Z=x,U=x²`, the three summands occupy exponent classes 0,1,2 modulo 3. Thus the substitution preserves injectivity. The leading three coefficients, at degrees 3n+2,3n+1,3n, are always 1,1,0, leaving a difference degree at most 3n−1. Under only `Z=x,U=x²`, the independent powers of x similarly separate f1,f2,f3; the common leading terms cancel and the total difference degree is at most n+1. A substitution is not generally injective; these disjoint exponent classes are the reason these particular ones are.
8. For eight lanes, deal N data pairs into fixed lanes; each lane has at most `ceil(N/8)` pairs. Combine lane results as `Σ_(j=0)^7 P_j w^j` using one additional independent uniform w. A differing lane supplies a nonzero coefficient polynomial in a **distinct power of w**, so cancellation between lanes cannot make the formal difference zero. Its total degree is at most `ceil(N/8)+7`, yielding that amount divided by q. Sharing u,y,z between lanes does not invalidate this polynomial argument. Empty lanes need a fixed, consistent initialization and the comparison is at the same encoded message length.

**The 89-bit row must be separated from this theorem.** The local table assigns five 64-bit state words, 88 score bits and a full 89-bit output to a timed Mersenne implementation. The gathered source provenance identifies `PaperMers89Smart`: a **two-key** form with x in `[0,p)`, y in `[0,2^63)`, 15-byte data steps, and a cast returning the **low 64 bits** of the final residue. It is not the three-uniform-field-key full-output family above. The five-word representation in the saved code is two 128-bit words plus one 64-bit word, consistent with that two-key variant; it does not identify a three-key implementation.

For the two-key restricted set, a conservative multivariate bound would use denominator `2^63`, not p, even before truncation, and its byte-to-pair encoding needs its own injectivity argument. The old comment's degree N+2 can be retained as a loose bound, but cannot justify an 89-bit-output score for this code. Its final truncation is not repaired by casually adding `2^-64` or multiplying an AU bound by `2^25`; §10 gives a concrete counterexample to that inference. The supplied paper already warns about the approximately `2^-25` wrap collision. In particular, **a 64-bit-output family admitting L=1 cannot score above 64 under the paper's own floor**, irrespective of its full-field intermediate result. The source/timing label must be fixed or the actual full-output implementation supplied before publication.

**3. Enumeration.** Over GF4, all 16 one-pair messages and 64 keys have max **1/4**, exactly 1/q. For all 256 two-pair messages and 64 keys, max is **7/16≤2/4**. The proof's decoder covers all fields, including characteristic 2; the enumerations corroborate rather than replace it.

**4. Score and verdict.** For fixed-length odd-word padding with `n=ceil(L/2)` in GF(2^64), **64 at L=1**: `L/ceil(L/2)≥1`. The literal even-word domain gives **65** at every even length before saturation. Eight lanes give **61 at L=1** because `ceil(ceil(L/2)/8)+7≤8L` and equality holds there. The single-key bound under the same padding convention scores **63 at L=1**; on even-only lengths its large-n ratio tends toward 2q/3, so the domain matters.

For three-key full-output `F_(2^89−1)`, the analogous padded bound scores **`log2 p≈89`** at L=1, and the even-only pair model scores **`1+log2 p≈90`**. Neither explains the table's 88. The loose **different two-key model**, full-field keys and 15-byte steps, would give `log2((15N/8)p/(N+2))`, minimized at N=1, approximately **88.3219**; changing the smaller key set to `2^63` lowers that pre-truncation calculation to about **62.3219**. These calculations are not a certificate for the timed/truncated code. **The three-key theorem and explicitly defined lanes hold. The 89-bit benchmark row could not be verified as labeled.**

## 14. ChainHash: complete audit of the stated composition

**Primary source:** [paper/appendix_chainhash.tex](paper/appendix_chainhash.tex), especially `thm:ph:collision`, `lem:ph:clnh`, `lem:ph:stream`, `lem:ph:injective`, `lem:ph:level2`, `lem:ph:chain`, `lem:ph:finalizer`, `lem:ph:twist`, and `rem:ph:gaps`; [paper/injective.tex](paper/injective.tex), `lem:injective:coeffmap`. These local files, not the earlier gathered excerpts, are the audited manuscript snapshot.

**1. Exact theorem and domain.** Use `F=F_2[X]/(X^64+X^4+X^3+X+1)`, q=`2^64`. Fix W 64-bit words per block, B=8W bytes, S∈{1,2} with 8S dividing W. Messages are byte strings of length **`0≤ℓ<2^64`**, as required explicitly in assumption A6. Set `n(m)=max(1,ceil(ℓ/B))` and `p(m)=S n(m)`. The key comprises W independent uniform κ words, three independent uniform recurrence keys u,y,z, five independent uniform circuit parameters c0,…,c4, and independent τ. Total **W+9 words**.

Each sub-block is zero padded only to its last 32-byte group; the fixed strided permutation pairs words `(w0,w2)` and `(w1,w3)`. The unreduced CLNH value is split into low/high 64-bit halves `(a_t,b_t)`. XOR the byte length into **both halves of the final stream pair**. Evaluate the three-key recurrence on this stream to obtain V. Add τ as an **integer modulo 2^64**, then apply

`G1=X²`, `G2=(G1+c0)(X+G1+c1)`, `f_c(X)=(X+c2)(G2+c3)+c4`.

The full 64-bit output is `f_c(V boxplus τ)`. For two distinct messages with at most n blocks each, put p=Sn. The theorem is **AU with ε=`(p+2)/q`**, capped at 1. No smaller seeded key space is the theorem's random key.

**2. Step-by-step proof.**

**A. Full-width CLNH, including different pair counts.** Equal pair counts use the integral-domain argument of §1: condition on all but one paired key, leaving `δk=C` for δ≠0, at most one solution among q choices. The argument works for every 128-bit C; no field reduction of the 128-bit stream is permitted here.

For w<w' and **C≠0**, append the extra pairs one at a time. At t=w, either the two prefixes differ, giving probability at most 1/q, or they agree, giving probability 0 because C≠0. For the next independent pair, its first operand is zero with probability 1/q. In that case the product vanishes and the previous target event remains, independently of this key. Otherwise multiplication by the nonzero first operand is injective in `F_2[X]`, so at most one second operand works. Thus

`p_t≤(1−1/q)/q + p_(t−1)/q ≤ 1/q`.

This closes the induction with the exact inequality, including degenerate keys. Without C≠0 it is false: empty versus one pair gives `2/q−1/q²` at target zero. The appendix explicitly includes this exception and handles it correctly.

**B. Distinct streams.** If message block counts differ, streams have different lengths and are distinct deterministically. Otherwise they have the same p. At equal byte length, padding/permutation is injective on the original bytes, so some sub-block differs at a fixed equal pair count. Full stream equality implies collision of that one block; probability ≤1/q. No multiplication across reused block keys is needed.

At unequal byte lengths and equal block count, equality of the last stream pair requires raw CLNH difference

`C=(ℓ xor ℓ')(1+X^64)`.

This is nonzero because the length encodings are distinct. If padded last sub-blocks are identical the equation is impossible. If their pair counts agree but contents differ, use case A's equal-count result. If pair counts differ, use case A's nonzero-target induction. Hence `Pr[streams equal]≤1/q`. This covers empty sub-blocks in the S=2 variant and the empty message. The fixed strided pairing is a bijective permutation of message and key positions and does not alter any key-independence argument.

**C. Recurrence.** Condition on a κ yielding different streams; u,y,z remain independent uniform. The decoder in §13 proves the formal stream-to-polynomial map injective. At equal stream lengths ≤p the difference degree is ≤p, so `Pr[V=V' | κ]≤p/q`. At unequal stream lengths it is ≤p+1, giving `(p+1)/q`. When stream lengths differ the stage-B failure event is impossible, which is why the extra degree does **not** require increasing the final global bound to `(p+3)/q`.

**D. Finalizer's coefficient bijection.** Let b=c0+c1 and d=c0c1, with all additions in characteristic 2. Direct expansion gives

`e5=1`, `e4=1+c2`, `e3=b+c2`, `e2=c0+c2 b`,
`e1=d+c3+c0 c2`, `e0=c4+c2(d+c3)`.

Given any desired `(e0,…,e4)`, recover in this order:

`c2=e4+1`, `b=e3+c2`, `c0=e2+c2 b`, `c1=b+c0`,
`d=c0c1`, `c3=e1+d+c0 c2`, `c4=e0+c2(d+c3)`.

Substituting each expression back recovers its row; conversely the same formulas recover every original parameter vector. All pivots are 1, so no inversion of a random value and no perfect-field assumption is needed. Uniform c therefore induces uniform **independent** coefficients e0,…,e4 of a monic degree-5 polynomial. The main paper's characteristic-zero/large-characteristic preprocessing theorem is not used; the appendix's separate characteristic-2 proof is sufficient.

**E. Finalizer collision and the twist.** For fixed v≠v', condition on all e_i except e1. The output difference is `(v−v')e1+C`. Its slope is nonzero in F, so exactly one of q e1 values causes equality. Thus collision probability is exactly 1/q, **not 5/q**. More generally the Vandermonde evaluation matrix has rank t on t≤5 distinct inputs, proving independent uniform outputs there. Degree 5 buys that conditional independence, not a degree factor in the pair collision bound.

For every fixed τ, integer addition modulo 2^64 is a bijection of the input words; its inverse is integer subtraction. It need not be field-affine. Conditional on κ,u,y,z,τ, the independent finalizer coefficients remain uniform and its two inputs are distinct exactly when V≠V'. Therefore the exact conditional 1/q bound remains valid. Randomness of τ is not needed for this collision argument, although it is part of the specified family and other design goals.

**F. Composition.** Partition collisions into `E1={streams equal}`, `E2={streams differ,V=V'}`, and `E3={V≠V',final outputs equal}`. These are disjoint. If block counts agree, their probabilities are at most `1/q`, `p/q`, `1/q`, respectively, totaling `(p+2)/q`. If counts differ, the bounds are `0`, `(p+1)/q`, `1/q`, totaling the **same** quantity. Averaging the conditional bounds is justified by independent keys between stages. No unjustified product of correlated events occurs.

**G. Scope and remaining gaps.** Assumption A6's `<2^64` length restriction must accompany the standalone theorem; its “at most nB bytes” paraphrase is within that domain. None of u,y,z needs to be nonzero; zero keys are included in the root count. The theorem's exact field operations, all output bits, and specific degree-5 circuit are essential. Truncating to t bits would require the new bound `Pr[V=V']+(1−Pr[V=V'])2^-t`, not the old 64-bit one.

Assumption A1 explicitly says the shipped code expands a single 64-bit seed with splitmix64. The resulting subfamily is **not proved** by the theorem; A2 explicitly says implementation equivalence is tested, not formally proved. This audit does not relabel those disclosures as verification. It also does not label the entire composed family exactly 5-independent: the appendix proves total-variation error at most `binom(t,2)(p+1)/q`, because intermediate collisions remain possible. Conditional 5-independence cannot be substituted without further analysis into a theorem requiring exact independence.

**3. Enumeration.** All 1024 circuit parameter vectors over GF4 produce all 1024 degree-5 lower-coefficient vectors exactly once; all distinct pairs of field inputs collide for exactly **256/1024=1/4** keys. The independent Rabin test verifies the actual degree-64 reduction polynomial.

A scaled two-word-block ChainHash analogue over GF8 exhausts all **32768** `(κ0,κ1,u,y,z)` keys and all **15** messages over a binary symbol alphabet of lengths 0–3. Its length word is inserted into both halves of the last pair. The maximum intermediate collision is **15/64**, below `(p+1)/8=3/8` for p≤2. Summing the independent monic finalizer keys exactly gives

`1/8 + (7/8)(15/64) = 169/512 < (p+2)/8 = 1/2`.

The finalizer coefficient bijection holds over GF8 by the algebraic proof, so the factored summation is exact; it is not a Monte Carlo step. This toy uses two-word blocks/adjacent pairs and a tiny length alphabet, not the production 32-byte grouping. Its domain and key sizes are recorded rather than presented as a byte-for-byte production test.

**4. Score and verdict.** For the stated theorem, on positive L:

- 1 KB: ε=`(2 ceil(L/128)+2)/q`, minimum **62 at L=1**.
- 256 B: ε=`(ceil(L/32)+2)/q`, minimum **`64−log2 3=62.4150375` at L=1**.
- 64 B: ε=`(ceil(L/8)+2)/q`, same minimum **62.4150375 at L=1**.

For W,S≥1, `ceil(L/W)≤L`, hence `S ceil(L/W)+2≤(S+2)L`; equality holds at L=1. This proves the minima directly, including block-boundary jumps. Empty-message handling uses `max(1,ceil(ℓ/B))`; replacing it blindly by zero at ℓ=0 is not the definition. **The stated ideal-key ChainHash collision bound holds. The paper's integer 62 scores are rounded; the two smaller-block configurations have the more precise values above. The shipped seed-expansion guarantee remains unverified.**

## 15. Poly1305 (additional record in `provable.json`)

**Primary source:** Bernstein, [The Poly1305-AES message-authentication code](https://cr.yp.to/mac/poly1305-20050329.pdf), 2005-03-29, §§2–3, Theorems 3.1–3.3; [saved complete text](gathered/poly1305-ghash/poly1305-20050329.txt). This section audits the hash inside Poly1305, not the separate AES/nonce security reduction.

**1. Statement.** Let `p=2^130−5`. Split a byte string into chunks of at most 16 bytes. Interpret each as a little-endian integer and add `2^(8·chunk_bytes)`; denote these nonzero coefficients by c1,…,cd. Define

`P_m(X)=c1 X^d+…+cd X`, and `H_r(m)=(P_m(r) mod p) mod 2^128`.

Choose r uniformly from the clamped subset R of size **2^106** (22 specified bits of a uniform 128-bit word are cleared). For distinct strings of at most n bytes and every fixed integer-additive difference modulo `2^128`, the probability is at most **`clip(8 ceil(n/16)/2^106)`**. Equality of the full 128-bit output is the zero-difference case. An independent fixed pad added modulo `2^128` preserves this equality bound. It does not turn an information-theoretic statement about R into a statement about every PRNG that generates keys.

**2. Re-derivation.**

1. Every marked coefficient lies strictly between 0 and `2^129`, hence below p. Different chunk counts leave a nonzero leading coefficient in `P_m−P_m'`. At equal counts, a zero difference polynomial implies equal coefficients modulo p, hence equal integers because their absolute differences are smaller than p. The position of the marker in the final coefficient recovers its exact byte length; the other coefficients then recover every byte. Thus the coefficient encoding is injective across lengths, including the empty string.
2. All data coefficients multiply **positive powers** of X. Subtracting any integer constant u cannot erase the nonzero data coefficient. Therefore `P_m−P_m'−u` is a nonzero polynomial over F_p of degree at most d=`ceil(n/16)`. Theorem 3.1 supplies a Pocklington primality certificate for p; the root argument uses that p is prime.
3. Two canonical field residues have ordinary integer difference in `−(p−1)…p−1`. Fixing their difference modulo `2^128` admits at most **8** integers in that interval: its width is less than `8·2^128`, and its inclusive length is `8·2^128−11`. For each lift u, step 2 allows at most d roots, including when roots are restricted to R. A union bound gives at most 8d bad keys among the **2^106** equally likely keys.
4. This is a valid truncation proof because **every lifted polynomial remains nonzero**. The corresponding condition fails for general unshifted polynomial AU (§10). The proof's displayed implication in Theorem 3.3 reverses the sign of g when it switches from `H(m)−H(m')` to `P_m'−P_m`; replacing g by −g fixes it without changing the bound, since all g are covered.
5. Clamping a uniform 128-bit word is uniform on R: every clamped value has exactly `2^22` preimages. Full-field sampling is unnecessary. A reused Poly1305 authentication key and a one-time MAC are different security settings; no claim about many-message forgery follows merely from this pairwise hash bound.

**3. Enumeration.** A scaled marked-block family uses `p=61`, 4 output bits, coefficients `{0}∪[16,31]` (empty string or one marked 4-bit chunk), and all 61 evaluation keys. The exact maximum over all 136 message pairs and all additive differences is **8/61**, attained by coefficients 30 and 31 at a differential target of 5. This exactly meets the eight-lift, degree-one bound in this toy. It does not replace the production clamped-key count.

**4. Score and verdict.** With n=8L, ε=`8 ceil(L/2)/2^106`. Since `ceil(L/2)≤L`, its score is **103 at L=1**. Probability clipping cannot lower that minimum. **The hash bound holds as stated, with the harmless sign correction to the proof.** The stronger-looking AES-MAC formula includes additional computational and nonce assumptions.

## 16. GHASH (additional record in `provable.json`)

**Primary source:** McGrew–Viega, [The Security and Performance of the Galois/Counter Mode (GCM) of Operation](https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/193), Appendix A, Lemma 2; [saved full text](gathered/poly1305-ghash/gcm-eprint-2004-193.txt). The standardized recurrence and encoding are also specified in [NIST SP 800-38D](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-38D), §§6.4 and 7.1.

**1. Correct statement.** Let H be uniform in `GF(2^128)` defined by `X^128+X^7+X^2+X+1`. For inputs (A,C), separately pad each bit string to a multiple of 128 bits, then append the 128-bit block containing their two 64-bit **bit lengths**. Each original bit length must be `<2^64` for this encoding argument. Starting Y0=0, apply `Y_i=(Y_(i−1) xor D_i)H` to the resulting d blocks. Distinct input pairs with at most d blocks have full-output **AXU bound `d/2^128`**. Truncating to t specified output bits gives `d/2^t` AXU, capped at 1.

For arbitrary two byte streams with total length at most 8L bytes, a valid common degree cap is **`d≤ceil(L/2)+2`**. If one stream is always empty, this improves to **`ceil(L/2)+1`**. The two-stream encoding and a single-stream polynomial hash must not share the latter bound without an additional argument.

**2. Re-derivation and errors in the cited proof.**

1. Expand the recurrence as `D1 H^d+…+Dd H`. For distinct pairs with different length tuples, the coefficient of H is the different length block, so their difference is nonzero. With equal length tuples, padding positions and block counts agree; some data block differs and supplies a nonzero coefficient at a positive power. This also proves injectivity when A and C contain zeros.
2. Subtracting any fixed full-width XOR target changes only the constant coefficient. The difference polynomial remains nonzero and has degree at most the greater block count. Root counting gives at most d bad H values among `2^128`. Irreducibility of the modulus is essential; it is independently checked in the executable suite.
3. A fixed t-bit output difference has exactly `2^(128−t)` possible full-width XOR lifts. Each is bounded by step 2. Union-bounding gives `d·2^(128−t)/2^128=d/2^t`. This projection is linear in the **same XOR group** as the proved differential property; it is not the invalid generic AU projection rule.
4. Let a,c be the two input lengths in bits. The true block count is `ceil(a/128)+ceil(c/128)+1`. The cited proof writes `(p+1)/2^128≤ceil((a+c)/128+1)/2^128`; that inequality is **false** for two independently partial blocks. For example A of one byte and C of seven bytes use two padded data blocks plus a length block, i.e. d=3, while its proposed right-hand degree is 2. The correct general inequality is `d≤ceil((a+c)/128)+2`. This refutes the proof's degree inequality; it does not claim that the particular short example attains three production-field roots.
5. The cited proof also says distinct pairs imply distinct concatenations A||C. They need not: different splits can have the same concatenation. The length block, as used in step 1, supplies the missing distinction. Its displayed polynomial equation omits the length-block term and uses powers inconsistent with its listed block order; use the expanded standardized recurrence above. Its root-count probabilities should be **≤**, not equality. None of these shortcuts should survive in a publication's proof transcription.
6. In GCM, H is `AES_K(0^128)`. Treating it as uniform is the ideal cipher/PRF model, not an information-theoretic proof of that value's distribution for the concrete AES key family. The GCM tag also includes a nonce-derived mask and can be truncated; its full adversarial security theorem is not the GHASH pair-collision theorem. No such MAC conclusion is asserted here.

**3. Enumeration.** Over GF16 with modulus `X^4+X+1`, let each of A,C contain zero or one field symbol; encode their lengths in one field symbol as `4 len(A)+len(C)`. All **289** input pairs, all 16 H values and all XOR targets give exact maximum **3/16**, matching the corrected d=3 bound. The production degree-128 modulus also passes the Rabin irreducibility test.

**4. Score and verdict.** For arbitrary two byte streams, `ceil(L/2)+2≤3L`, so the corrected bound scores **`128−log2 3=126.4150375` at L=1**. For a single stream, it scores **127 at L=1**. For a t-bit truncation of the two-stream version the analogous score is `t−log2 3` when `t≥2`; clipping handles smaller outputs. **The root/lifting theorem holds with the corrected length accounting and polynomial expression.** The earlier 127-bit score needs the single-stream restriction.
